# The 2-minute pitch (Barcelona demo day)

Hackathon pitches are short. You're in front of judges who've seen 30 pitches already. You have 120 seconds to make them remember yours.

## Structure

| Segment | Time | Goal |
|--|--|--|
| Hook | 30s | Make them care about **one specific user**. |
| Demo | 60s | Show the gasp live. |
| Moat + ask | 30s | Why this is hard to copy + what you want from them. |

## The hook (30 seconds)

Name the user. Describe one specific pain moment. Do not start with *"in a world where..."* or *"today's landscape of..."* — judges tune those out in the first 5 seconds.

Kokoro example:

> *"Meet Sam. 32, running a SaaS company. Tonight he's lying awake rehearsing tomorrow's Series A pitch. He's wired, he's scattered, and the meditation app on his phone won't help because it doesn't know the pitch. Watch."*

Rules:

- Name the user aloud — "Sam," not "a user."
- Describe ONE specific moment (not five use cases).
- End with a tee-up that cuts directly to the demo. ("Watch." "Here's what happens.")

## The demo (60 seconds)

This is the heart. Show the gasp live. **No slides during this segment.**

Rules:

- You drive, narrating briefly. ("Sam types what's on his mind. Three words. Watch.")
- The input is live and minimal. One sentence of typing, not a form.
- The gasp happens on-screen. Judges should react audibly.
- Do not explain the tech stack.
- If something breaks: don't apologize for 20 seconds. Say *"one sec, let me restart that"* and continue. Rehearse the recovery.

## The moat + ask (30 seconds)

**Moat in one sentence.** What's the unfair thing about what you built?

For Kokoro: *"We don't generate content — we earn the right to show up at 6:45am with something specific enough that Sam opens it instead of dismissing it. That's the moat: specificity at the moment of delivery."*

**The ask.** Judges want to know what to do next. Options:

- *"Come to our booth — we want your feedback on the voice."*
- *"We're looking for Sam himself. If that's you or someone you know, find us."*
- *"We'd love an intro to [specific named person/company] if you know them."*

Never end with *"thanks for listening."* End with the ask.

## Anti-patterns (all common, all kill)

- **Problem slide that takes 45 seconds.** Judges understand problems. Move faster.
- **Team slide.** Nobody cares at a hackathon. Put names on the product itself.
- **Tech stack slide.** Judges assume you built it. *"We used Next.js and Prisma"* wastes 15 seconds.
- **Competitive grid.** You're not raising a seed. Show why *you* are the one building this, not who else isn't.
- **Recorded demo.** Live every time. If live terrifies you, you've over-scoped. Fix that, not the recording.
- **"First let me show you the sign-up flow."** No. Sign-up is off-path. Demo starts at the gasp.
- **"It would do X in production."** Never. Only show what it does *right now*.

## Before the pitch (T-minus 2 hours)

- Practice 10 times. Out loud. Timed on a phone stopwatch.
- Memorize the first sentence and the last sentence. Middle can be looser.
- Decide your fallback if the demo crashes. Usually: a 20-second phone video as last resort. Rehearse deploying it.
- Eat. Drink water. You will be more nervous than you think.

## After the pitch

Judges will approach you at the booth. Have a one-liner ready:

> *"We're at booth 14. Takes 30 seconds to try it with your own input — want to see it?"*

Get judges to touch the product themselves. A judge who types their own sentence into Kokoro and hears their own audio ritual remembers you. A judge who only watched the demo, doesn't.
